Purpose

eWatts was created to be a neutral bridge, not to replace existing monetary systems, but to complement them.

Each form of money in use today solves a specific problem well. What is missing is a layer where strangers can exchange value on symmetric terms, without intermediaries, with backing in physical work.

We believe that fair and transparent money can serve as a foundation for greater global collaboration, trust, and economic stability.

What is eWatts

There are roughly four ways to store and move value today.

Fiat liquidates commerce globally, but supply expands by policy, silently devaluing principal. Gold has preserved purchasing power through physical production cost, but is slow and expensive to transact and prone to synthetic expansion in paper markets. Bitcoin solved digital scarcity and portability, but transactions are public, and emission is capped by schedule rather than the energy actually spent on mining.

Each resolved the problem it was built for.

eWatts borrows from each. Supply expands continuously at real energy cost, like gold. It is divisible and decentralized, like Bitcoin. But mining is bound to DRAM latency, which is a physical property that has been essentially flat for two decades (1-2% improvement per year, versus 20-30% for ASICs). No specialized hardware can shortcut it. A computer from five years ago mines at the same efficiency as one built today.

Two algorithms, two roles. Mining is deliberately slow and energy-intensive, anchoring each coin to measurable physical work. Transactions are private and quickly verified in under three milliseconds, with sender, receiver, and amount hidden by default.

No pre-mine. No token sale. No founder allocation. No company. Mining is the only way to acquire new coins.

The unit is calibrated as a reference, not a promise. Two decimal places, like traditional money.

Protocol

Core design

Mining runs on standard RAM

No ASICs, no GPUs required. Any computer with RAM can mine — a machine from five years ago performs at the same efficiency as one built today. Explore the economic model.

Private by default

Sender, recipient, and amount are hidden. Each transaction uses ring signatures (11 possible keys — an observer can't tell which is yours), one-time stealth addresses (no two payments to the same person can be linked), and Pedersen commitments that hide amounts while keeping total supply auditable.

Money supply follows energy cost

New coins are emitted proportionally to total network bandwidth. More miners means more emission, but each coin costs energy to mine. The emission formula anchors each eWatt to the energy cost of production, a universal physical unit.

Governance designed for stability

Major changes require 95% of miners and 95% of nodes. Governance evolves from founder-led to technical maintainers to community stewardship as the network grows.
