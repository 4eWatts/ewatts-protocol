A fair means of exchange.
Backed by energy, secured by memory.

eWatts is a digital currency designed to be fair by default. Fair to mine (any computer can do it). Fair to value (backed by the energy cost of production). Fair to transact (private from the start, not as an afterthought).

The name comes from Watt, the unit of power — because each coin is referenced to the energy cost of mining it. Not arbitrary. Physics-based.

An eWatt is a ruler, not a bet. Each coin is measurable energy — you can verify the cost of mining it. No speculation required.

Why it matters

A more honest store of energy

Bridges for global collaboration

Trade across borders is friction by design — capital controls, currency conversion, settlement delays. A neutral medium of exchange reduces this friction to near zero. eWatts is designed to serve as that bridge: permissionless to use, private by default, and anchored to a real cost of production rather than to the policy of any country. Not a replacement for local currency. A settlement layer for trade that should not require permission.

Gold but accessible

Gold has a cost floor (expensive to mine), does not degrade, and has served as money for millennia. But gold is difficult to divide, heavy to transport, and requires physical vaults for storage. eWatts preserves the cost floor — energy, the only commodity available in every country — and removes the physical limitations: perfectly divisible into 100 ecents (two decimals — familiar to anyone who uses dollars), transmissible at the speed of light, storable as a 32-byte private key. And unlike gold, mining runs on standard memory, with no specialized hardware required.

Why DRAM?

Most proof-of-work currencies use hash functions (SHA-256, Ethash) that can be accelerated by specialized hardware — ASICs and GPUs. This concentrates mining power into factories over time. eWatts uses a different primitive: random access to a large dataset held in DRAM. The mining bottleneck is memory bandwidth, not computation. The ratio of bandwidth to energy in DRAM has remained stable across multiple process generations. No ASIC can escape this physical limit. This makes DRAM the hardest substrate to monopolize — precisely because it is the most common.

Grounded in energy, not a stablecoin

Miners cannot profitably sell below their energy cost. New miners enter when price exceeds it. Over time, the market oscillates around this energy floor — not a peg, but a physical boundary. The network's Value of Resource (kWh per coin) is visible on every block. There is no halving schedule because supply responds to actual bandwidth demand. Like gold and Bitcoin, eWatts is a commodity money — suited as a settlement layer and store of value, not as a day-to-day medium of exchange with stable purchasing power. This is not a limitation. It is the property of any money that cannot be arbitrarily printed.

Private from the start, not an option

Every transaction preserves privacy by design: stealth addresses protect the recipient, ring signatures protect the sender, and Pedersen commitments protect the amount. No selective disclosure. Privacy is the only mode supported. Changing this requires near-unanimous network consent — 95% of miners and 95% of nodes via hard fork.

Protocol

Architecture

eWatts is built around a simple idea: make mining accessible to anyone with a computer. The algorithm is designed to run fastest on standard RAM — not on specialized hardware. Transactions are private by default, and the network has no central authority, no admin keys, and no foundation.

Mining runs on standard RAM

No ASICs, no GPUs required. Mining speed is determined by how fast your computer can read its own memory. Every computer has RAM. Every computer can mine. The algorithm performs random memory accesses that scale with bandwidth, not clock speed.

Your identity is protected

Each transaction is signed with a ring of 11 possible keys. An observer cannot tell which key produced the signature. The same key can be used multiple times without linking the transactions together.

Transaction amounts are private

The value of each transaction is committed cryptographically, visible only to the parties involved. Range proofs prove the amount is positive without revealing it. The total supply remains auditable by summing commitments on-chain.

Every payment gets a unique address

Each transaction creates a one-time destination known only to the recipient. No two payments to the same person can be linked. Only the recipient can prove they received it.

Money supply follows energy cost

New coins are emitted proportionally to total network bandwidth. More miners means more emission, but each coin costs energy to mine. The target is approximately 1 USD per eWatt at 2026 energy costs — a reference for understanding, not a hard peg. Each eWatt divides into 100 ecents (eWatt-cents). No satoshis. No mental math.

No one controls the network

No developer fund. No admin keys. No foundation. No CEO. Protocol changes require 95% of miners and 95% of nodes. The rules are enforced by code, not by people.

Get started

Mine in 5 minutes

You need a computer with Rust installed. Minimum 4 GB of free DRAM. No GPU, no ASIC, no token purchase.

# Install Rust
$ curl --proto '=https' --tlsv1.2 -sSf https://sh.rustup.rs | sh

# Clone
$ git clone https://github.com/4Ewatts/ewatts-protocol.git
$ cd ewatts-protocol
$ cargo build --release

# Initialize state and mine
$ ./target/release/ewatts-protocol init
$ ./target/release/ewatts-protocol mine

# Start dashboard on port 8080
$ ./target/release/ewatts-protocol dash
